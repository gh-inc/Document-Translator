# DT-108 — protect untranslated PDF source from overlapping redactions

> **Status:** implemented and verified on 2026-10-04. Ticket `DT-108`, stage 11.
> Outcome recorded at the end of this document.
> Origin and evidence: [PDF layout root-cause execution](2026-10-04-pdf-layout-root-cause-execution.md).
> Scope is deliberately limited to `app/adapters/formats/pdf.py` and
> `tests/adapters/formats/test_pdf_layout_overflow_probe.py`. No REST, MCP,
> domain-model or database contract changes.

## The defect

The renderer keeps untranslated source text on the page and removes source text
where it draws a translation. It already has a protection mechanism
(`protected_by_page`, `pdf.py:173`) for blocks that stay as source, but a block
with **no translation at all** takes the `continue` at `pdf.py:175` before its
rectangle is ever registered. Extracted block bboxes overlap — the code's own
comment at `pdf.py:198` says so — so a neighbour's redaction rectangle wipes
that source text. Nothing raises, nothing is logged, `RenderResult` does not
report it. Text silently disappears from the output PDF.

Confirmed instance (`test_pdf_layout_overflow_probe.py:57`): `seq=4`
(`' Ключевые идеи\n\x01🏛️\x02'`) and `seq=5` (`'• (•):Мир идей эйдосов'`) have
intersecting bboxes; with `seq=4` untranslated, the output keeps
`'Plato argued'` and loses `'Ключевые идеи'`.

## Why the first proposed fix was rejected

Shrinking the protected rectangle was proposed, then measured and rejected.

Adjacent-block intersections on `samples/platon-gliph.pdf`, 3 pages, 70 blocks:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---:|
| Adjacent pairs intersecting | 49 of 69 |
| Median intersection height | 2.54 pt |
| Maximum intersection height | 16.34 pt |
| Block heights observed | 16.3 – 47.5 pt |

Two cases make a constant inset unusable:

- `#26` (`'Проект идеального'`, height 16.34 pt) is contained in `#27`
  (`'научиться Зрелый …'`, height 47.54 pt) over its **full height**. Removing
  the intersection would require shrinking the block to nothing.
- The confirmed `seq=4` / `seq=5` case intersects by 7.10 pt while the
  `'Ключевые идеи'` span ends at y=252.85 and the block ends at y=257.92 — at
  most **5.07 pt** of bottom inset is tolerable. A larger inset unprotects real
  glyphs; the median 2.54 pt inset does not remove the 7.10 pt intersection.

The intersection is inflated by an oversized span, not by the text: the `🏛️`
span occupies `y 234.13 → 257.92` while the text span occupies
`y 235.77 → 252.85`. Shrinking protection would trade phantom leftovers for
real text loss on the very case DT-108 exists to fix.

## Decision — clip redactions by foreign spans, not by shrink

Fix the **redaction** area rather than the protection. A block's own characters
always lie inside its own spans, so subtracting every foreign span from the
redaction rectangle guarantees that no foreign character is removed, while the
block's own characters remain inside the redaction and are removed.

Measured precondition: `#26` is nested in `#27` **vertically only**; the
intersection is 66.52 pt of `#26`'s 119.76 pt width, so subtraction leaves a
non-empty remainder in both directions.

The adapter is therefore given one `get_text("dict")` call per page, and the
redaction for block *N* becomes *N*.rect minus every span on that page not
mostly contained in *N*.

`protected_by_page` and `_unprotected_rectangles` are then redundant: a
degraded block's spans are foreign to every other block, so the span rule
protects them too. This removes the asymmetry recorded in the root-cause
execution — degraded blocks were protected with a full rectangle (already
carrying phantom-leftover risk), untranslated blocks with none.

Both symbols are referenced only inside `pdf.py`, so the replacement is local.

## Steps

1. **Red test.** Invert the characterization assertion in
   `test_missing_translation_can_erase_overlapping_source_block` from
   `not in` to `in`. It must fail against current code. The docstring changes
   from "record the known gap" to the desired behaviour.
2. **Containment test.** Add a sibling test for the `#26`/`#27` pair: when
   `#26` is untranslated and `#27` is translated, `"Проект идеального"` must
   survive. This is the case a constant inset cannot solve.
3. **Metrics guard.** Assert the intersection facts (49 of 69 intersecting
   pairs, median 2.54 pt, maximum 16.34 pt) so a future PyMuPDF change that
   alters segmentation fails loudly instead of silently changing coverage.
4. **Implementation.** Add `_page_foreign_spans` and `_redaction_rectangles`
   in `pdf.py`; use them in place of `_unprotected_rectangles`; drop
   `protected_by_page`.
5. **Verification.** `make test`, `make lint`, `make typecheck`.
   `test_pdf.py::test_pdf_neighboring_redaction_preserves_overlapping_degraded_block`
   must stay green — it is the DT-77 regression guard for the degraded path.

## Risks

- **Cost.** One `get_text("dict")` per rendered page, plus a per-block scan of
  that page's spans. Acceptable for linear documents; to be measured on a large
  sample before considering it a bottleneck.
- **Span-to-block ambiguity.** A span straddling two block rectangles matches
  neither at 90 % containment and is treated as foreign to both, which
  protects it. That is the safe direction: it cannot delete text.
- **Behaviour change for degraded blocks.** Their protection becomes
  span-scoped rather than full-rectangle, so a sliver of the bleed area around
  them is now redacted. Step 5 exists to prove no visible change.
- **Pages without spans.** If `get_text("dict")` returns nothing for a page
  that still has blocks, no foreign span exists and redaction covers the whole
  rectangle, matching today's behaviour for untranslated blocks.

## Out of scope

- The `RenderResult.fallback_blocks` / `fallback_pages` observability gap
  recorded in `DECISIONS.md` §5.
- Per-block overflow appending pages (DT-104 root cause, 42 of 70 blocks).
- Replacing `_unprotected_rectangles` for any non-PDF format.
## Outcome

Implemented as planned, with two corrections found during execution.

**`_page_spans` / `_is_owned_by` / `_subtract_rectangles` / `_redaction_rectangles`
replace `_unprotected_rectangles`.** A block's redaction is now clipped against
every page span the block does not own, where ownership means the rectangle
contains at least 90% of the span.

**Correction 1 — a first ownership implementation was wrong.** An earlier draft
assigned each span a single owning block by index and skipped the block being
redacted, so `_owner_index` could never return the owner. Every span was then
classified foreign, every redaction was fully subtracted, and nothing was
redacted at all. The DT-108 tests still passed, because they only asserted that
source text *survives*. Ownership had to be judged per span against the single
rectangle in question, and the tests had to assert both directions.

**Correction 2 — degraded blocks needed their stronger guarantee back.** With
span-scoped clipping alone, `test_pdf.py::test_pdf_neighboring_redaction_preserves_overlapping_degraded_block`
failed: that test gives a translated block a rectangle spanning the whole page,
so a degraded neighbour's span is ≥90% inside it and gets erased. Span clipping
therefore cannot replace whole-rectangle protection for degraded blocks, and
`protected_by_page` is retained for that case. The risk anticipated in this plan
materialised and was handled.

**Tests are two-sided.** Both DT-108 tests assert that the untranslated block's
source survives, that the neighbour's own Russian source is gone, and that the
neighbour's translation is present. A one-sided assertion would pass with
redaction disabled, which is exactly the false green that correction 1 exposed.
Verified by running them against the pre-fix `pdf.py`: both fail.

**`test_block_intersection_geometry_is_unchanged`** pins 49 intersecting
adjacent pairs, median 2.54 pt and maximum 16.34 pt, so a PyMuPDF upgrade that
changes segmentation fails loudly instead of silently changing coverage.

Final verification: `make test` **714 passed, 2 live deselected**; `make lint`
clean (188 files); `make typecheck` clean (61 source files).

Not addressed here, by agreement: the fallback observability gap in
`DECISIONS.md` §5, and per-block overflow appending pages. DT-108 removes a
silent text-loss defect; it does not reduce the 45-page output.
